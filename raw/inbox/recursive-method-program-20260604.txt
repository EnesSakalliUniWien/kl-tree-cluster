You should treat these as one program with four layers:

1. Calibration law: selected-tail external law plus stricter internal support.
2. Projection/feature-space law: selected MP, k_{\min}, sibling projection dimension, categorical/continuous covariance.
3. Traversal/FDR law: selected reachable-frontier BH and pass-through replacement.
4. Manuscript/evidence law: locked simulations, constants, real-data manifest, and selected-region geometry.

No paper I found solves the whole KL-TE problem end-to-end, because your pipeline combines same-data hierarchy construction, Tree-BH edge opening, selected PCA/MP dimension, sibling empirical inflation, sibling BH, and traversal. But several papers solve subproblems that can be used directly.

A. Papers to anchor the work

Selective inference after clustering: closest to your selected-hierarchy problem

The most relevant paper is Gao, Bien, and Witten, “Selective Inference for Hierarchical Clustering.” It directly addresses the fact that classical tests are anti-conservative when clusters are formed by hierarchical clustering, and proposes selective p-values that condition on the clustering selection event. This should be your primary conceptual model for Q5 and Q42–Q44.  ￼

For broader clustering selection, use Chen and Witten, “Selective inference for k-means clustering,” Chen and Gao, “Testing for a difference in means of a single feature after clustering,” and Yun and He, “Selective inference for multiple pairs of clusters after K-means clustering.” These papers are useful because they separate pairwise cluster testing from multiple pair testing, which maps well to your local sibling tests versus traversal/frontier multiplicity.  ￼

For a different clustering objective, Bachoc, Maugis-Rabusseau, and Neuvial, “Selective inference after convex clustering with \ell_1 penalization,” gives a polyhedral selective-inference route for convex clustering. It will not plug into average linkage directly, but it is useful for thinking about selection regions and path constraints.  ￼

For general selected-inference theory, use Fithian, Sun, and Taylor, “Optimal Inference After Model Selection,” and Lee, Sun, Sun, and Taylor, “Exact post-selection inference, with application to the lasso.” Fithian et al. frame the correct target as selective Type-I error, while Lee et al. provide the polyhedral conditioning template.  ￼

For the non-polyhedral/curvature direction, Tian et al., “Selective sampling after solving a convex problem,” is especially useful: it uses change-of-measure ideas and connects selective-inference Jacobians to curvature and Weyl–Steiner tube formulae. That is directly relevant to your root selected-region lift, where merge cells, edge-open cells, and PCA/MP cells are not all simple polyhedra.  ￼

Hierarchical FDR and traversal

For Tree-BH and hierarchical hypotheses, use Bogomolov et al., “Testing hypotheses on a tree: new error rates and controlling strategies.” This is the direct TreeBH reference and gives you the right language for tree-structured error targets.  ￼

For general hierarchical FDR alternatives, use Lynch and Guo, “On Procedures Controlling the FDR for Testing Hierarchically Ordered Hypotheses.” This is useful when comparing traversal-aligned sibling BH to other hierarchical FDR procedures.  ￼

For pass-through and gatekeeping logic, use Goeman and Solari, “The sequential rejection principle of familywise error control.” It provides a way to reason about graph/tree-gate procedures through monotonicity and sequential rejection, which is the right proof style if you replace pass-through with a descendant-vs-local rule.  ￼

MP, PCA, angles, and selected spectral geometry

For MP/PCA validation, the relevant base is the Marchenko–Pastur law and spiked covariance literature. Baik, Ben Arous, and Péché characterize the phase transition of the largest eigenvalue in spiked covariance models; this is useful for distinguishing weak spikes that should be missed from strong spikes that should enter the projection.  ￼

For largest eigenvalue finite-sample corrections, Tracy–Widom/Johnstone-type work is the right family. The search result here surfaced Soshnikov’s universality note, which describes Tracy–Widom limits for largest Wishart eigenvalues and extends the universality picture.  ￼

For deformed/non-identity MP, use Silverstein-style Stieltjes-transform support equations. A recent Bai–Hu–Silverstein–Zhou paper summarizes the “information-plus-noise” Stieltjes-transform/support route, which is close to what you need if local categorical/continuous spectra are not identity-whitened.  ￼

For PCA stability and angular rules, use Davis–Kahan/Wedin \sin\Theta perturbation theory. Modern high-dimensional versions include Cai and Zhang, “Rate-Optimal Perturbation Bounds for Singular Subspaces,” and O’Rourke, Vu, and Wang, “Matrices with Gaussian noise: optimal estimates for singular subspace perturbation.” These give a principled role to eigengap, spectral norm noise, and subspace angle.  ￼

For cone/tube geometry, Amelunxen, Lotz, McCoy, and Tropp, “Living on the edge,” gives statistical dimension and conic intrinsic-volume tools. This is relevant to selected-region tangent cones and angular cap probabilities.  ￼

Continuous covariance and p\gg n

For continuous p\gg n, dense empirical covariance should be replaced or augmented by shrinkage/low-rank methods. Ledoit and Wolf, “Nonlinear shrinkage estimation of large-dimensional covariance matrices,” is directly relevant because it targets large covariance matrices where the sample covariance performs poorly or is ill-conditioned.  ￼

For factor-plus-sparse residual structure, use POET/S-POET style methods. The Fan–Wang result surfaced here discusses ultra-high-dimensional spiked covariance, eigenvalue/eigenvector bias, and S-POET as a shrinkage principal orthogonal complement thresholding estimator.  ￼

Phylogenetic covariance/nulls

For phylogenetic selected tails, start from Felsenstein’s phylogenetically independent contrasts: the key idea is that related taxa are not independent, and Brownian-motion phylogenetic covariance can transform contrasts into independent standardized quantities.  ￼

For distance-based objects, distance-based MANOVA/DBF/PERMANOVA-like work is relevant because it separates vector observations from general dissimilarity objects. Minas and Montana’s DBF paper is a useful reference for approximate inference with distance-based statistics.  ￼

Q5. Fit and validate a selected-tail law

The law should not be “\chi^2 times scalar c” unless it passes a tail-shape test. The right response variable is already natural in your diagnostics:

R_u=\frac{T_u}{a_u\nu_u},

where T_u is the raw selected sibling statistic, a_u is the projected-Wald reference scale, and \nu_u is degrees of freedom. Your external diagnostic already checks scalar c versus a full selected-ratio tail law and marks non-admissible rows undefined.  ￼

Use a two-stage law:

\log R_u
=
m(x_u)+\varepsilon_u,

where

x_u=
\left[
A_{\mathrm{edge}},
\log(n_u/n),
\log(p/n_u),
k_u,
f_u,
\log(\lambda_{k,u}/\lambda_{+,u}),
M_{k,u},
\rho_u
\right].

Here A_{\mathrm{edge}} is edge severity, k_u is sibling projection dimension, f_u is feature family, \lambda_{k,u}/\lambda_{+,u} is selected eigenvalue over MP edge, M_{k,u} is selected eigenvalue mass, and \rho_u=\cos^2\theta_u is angular capture. This matches the repo’s geometry diagnostic, which already records edge p-values, parent size/depth, projection dimension, eigenvalue summaries, and angular alignment.  ￼

Validation target:

\Pr_{\mathrm{heldout}}\{R_u > q_{0.99}(x_u)\mid x_u\}\approx 0.01.

Do not validate by R^2 alone. Use held-out exceedance at \alpha_{\mathrm{sib}}=0.01, leave-one-case, leave-one-source-family, and leave-one-simulation. The selected-tail domain script already computes admissibility using matching simulations, matched records, held-out exceedance rate, and held-out exceedance SE.  ￼

Production admissibility should require:

m_{\mathrm{sim}}\ge 499,\qquad
m_{\mathrm{records}}\ge 499,\qquad
\mathrm{SE}_{\mathrm{tail}}\le 0.2\alpha_{\mathrm{sib}},

plus a predeclared context. The 499 rule comes from 1/(m+1)\le 0.2\alpha at \alpha=0.01.  ￼

Practical model sequence:

1. Exact bins first: feature family/source family, parent-size bin, edge-action bin, k.
2. Monotone quantile model next: q_{0.99}(x) using edge severity and parent size as monotone effects.
3. Spectral correction layer: add \lambda_k/\lambda_+, selected eigenvalue mass, effective rank.
4. Angular layer: add \rho=\cos^2\theta, but only if it improves leave-one-family tail calibration.
5. Never promote data-adaptive bins unless they are frozen before the validation run.

M_{\mathrm{desc}}(v)

>

Q42–Q44. Full selected-region law

Define the selected event for a focal sibling u:

\mathcal S_u
=
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{tree}}
\cap
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{TreeBH\ path}}
\cap
\mathcal P_{\mathrm{PCA/MP}}
\cap
\mathcal F_{\mathrm{sibling\ frontier}}
\cap
\mathcal I_{\mathrm{inflation/FDR}}
\cap
\mathcal U_{\mathrm{focal\ selection}}.

Then the target law is:

\mathcal L
\left(
R_u
\mid
X\in\mathcal S_u
\right).

Build it in layers:

Layer 1: root fixed-membership identity

Use the barycentric identity. For a binary parent with children L,R, n=n_L+n_R, parent mean

\bar x_u=\frac{n_L\bar x_L+n_R\bar x_R}{n},

and no branch scaling, the child-parent and sibling standardized contrasts satisfy

z_{L\to u}^{\mathrm{edge}}
=
z_{L,R}^{\mathrm{sib}},
\qquad
z_{R\to u}^{\mathrm{edge}}
=
-z_{L,R}^{\mathrm{sib}},

provided the covariance baseline is the same parent/pooled baseline. This explains why root edge opening and sibling statistic are geometrically coupled.

Layer 2: projection/angle law

For a projection P_k,

T_u=\|P_k z_u\|^2
=
\|z_u\|^2\cos^2\theta_u,

where

\cos^2\theta_u
=
\frac{\|P_k z_u\|^2}{\|z_u\|^2}.

This is the main trigonometric rule to add to the selected-tail law.

Under a fixed independent random subspace null,

\cos^2\theta \sim \mathrm{Beta}\left(\frac{k}{2},\frac{d-k}{2}\right),

and

\frac{(\cos^2\theta/k)}
{((1-\cos^2\theta)/(d-k))}
\sim F_{k,d-k}.

This gives a baseline angular diagnostic: if observed selected \rho=\cos^2\theta is far above the Beta/F reference, selected PCA alignment is part of the inflation law.

Layer 3: edge opening as radial truncation

For edge statistic Q,

r_{\mathrm{edge}}
=
\sqrt Q-\sqrt{q_{1-\alpha,k}},

and

A_{\mathrm{edge}}
=
-\log p_{\mathrm{edge,min}}.

Use r_{\mathrm{edge}} for local smooth geometry and A_{\mathrm{edge}} for robust binned selected-tail contexts.

Layer 4: selected PCA stability

Use Davis–Kahan/Wedin:

\|\sin\Theta(\hat V,V)\|
\lesssim
\frac{\|E\|}{\mathrm{eigengap}}.

So eigengap, selected eigenvalue over MP edge, and effective rank should enter the selected law as stability variables. This is exactly where trigonometry helps: \sin\Theta, \cos^2\theta, and \tan^2\theta=(1-\rho)/\rho are interpretable geometry variables. Davis–Kahan/Wedin-style perturbation papers justify this angle/eigengap link.  ￼

Layer 5: merge-region geometry

For average linkage, each selected merge has inequalities of the form

d(A,B)\le d(C,D)

against competing merges. In smooth Euclidean cases, approximate each active inequality by signed distance:

\delta_t
=
\frac{g_t(X)}{\|\nabla g_t(X)\|_{\Sigma_0^{-1}}},

then collect the minimum signed distance, active constraint count, and tangent cone. For discrete Hamming/categorical cases, treat the region as a tie-cell rather than a smooth manifold.

For non-root contexts, add ancestor path constraints:

\mathcal E_{\mathrm{anc}}(u)
=
\bigcap_{a\in\mathrm{path}(u)}
\{p_{a\to c}^{\mathrm{TreeBH}}\le \alpha_a\}.

Then fit the selected-tail law conditional on:

root/non-root
ancestor edge action
minimum ancestor radial margin
local edge action
parent size
projection dimension
spectral geometry
angular capture

C. Trigonometric rules worth adding now

These are the most useful “rules” to implement as diagnostics and candidate law variables.

1. Projection capture

\rho_u=\cos^2\theta_u
=
\frac{\|P_k z_u\|^2}{\|z_u\|^2}.

Use rho_u as angular capture. If \rho_u is high, the selected PCA basis is aligned with the sibling contrast.

2. Orthogonal leakage

1-\rho_u=\sin^2\theta_u.

This measures how much sibling energy lies outside the selected projection.

3. Tangent inflation score

\tan^2\theta_u=\frac{1-\rho_u}{\rho_u}.

Very small \tan^2\theta means the selected projection almost captures the entire contrast. That should predict larger selected-tail inflation.

4. Edge/sibling barycentric identity

z_{L\to u}=z_{L,R},\qquad z_{R\to u}=-z_{L,R}.

This is crucial because it says edge opening and sibling evidence are not independent at a binary parent. It explains why edge action is such a strong selected-tail covariate.

5. Two-edge law of cosines

When edge vectors are not exactly opposite because of different covariance/projection/context choices, use

\|z_L-z_R\|^2
=
\|z_L\|^2+\|z_R\|^2
-
2\|z_L\|\|z_R\|\cos\phi.

This separates sibling energy into edge magnitudes and edge angle. It can help distinguish “two children both far from parent in same direction” from “children separated from each other.”

6. Spherical cap baseline

For fixed independent subspace,

\Pr(\rho\ge r)

is a spherical-cap tail. Use it as an angular p-diagnostic, not a production p-value. If selected hierarchy produces many tiny cap p-values under null, angular selection is part of the law.

7. Cone angle / statistical dimension

Approximate a selected region near the observed point by a cone C_u. Its statistical dimension \delta(C_u) acts like an effective number of selected directions. This connects your selected-region geometry to conic integral geometry.  ￼

D. Execution order

Do this in the following order:

1. Internal thresholds: Q9–Q11 first, because they protect production now.
2. Selected-tail law: Q5 next, but keep it diagnostic until admissibility passes.
3. Projection validation: Q14, Q15, Q17, because they affect all p-values.
4. Categorical/continuous attribution: Q19–Q21, because feature-family failures should not be misattributed to FDR or calibration.
5. Traversal/FDR/pass-through: Q24–Q28, after local p-values are trustworthy.
6. Phylogenetic generator: Q29 before any precomputed-distance selected-tail claim.
7. Manuscript evidence lock: Q33–Q41 only after the above studies have stable manifests.
8. Full selected-region law: Q42–Q44 as the long-term proof/modeling track.

The single most important methodological choice is: external selected-tail calibration may enter production only as a predeclared, context-admissible tail law, not as a scalar fallback.
