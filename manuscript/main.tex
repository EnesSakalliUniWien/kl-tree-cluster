\title{KL-TE: Statistically Filtered Hierarchical Clustering with Projected-Wald Tests}
\author{Draft workspace}

\begin{abstract}
Hierarchical clustering exposes many candidate splits, but a geometric tree
cut does not say which splits are statistically credible. KL-TE is a
statistical filtering method for hierarchical clustering on typed
sample-feature data. The current implementation supports Bernoulli coordinates,
categorical one-hot blocks with a drop-last multinomial covariance model, and
continuous empirical-Gaussian blocks. It builds a candidate hierarchy,
summarizes each subtree by its empirical feature distribution, and annotates
candidate splits with two tests. The child-parent edge test asks whether a child
departs from its parent under the relevant feature-space covariance model. The
sibling test asks whether the two children under the same parent are distinct
after accounting for tree-selection scale distortion with a context-weighted
empirical-null inflation estimate. A top-down traversal then turns the
corrected annotations into terminal clusters. This manuscript records the
implemented method and the validation gaps that must be closed before
submission-level calibration or power claims can be made.
\end{abstract}
