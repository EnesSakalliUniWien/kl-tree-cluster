# phylo_large Adaptive pydiffmap Focus Audit 2026-06-27

## Summary

A focused rerun audited `tbs_diffusion_adaptive` on `phylo_large_32taxa` and
`phylo_large_64taxa`, including assignments, true-label crosstabs, p-value
traces, and raw/adaptive distance diagnostics. The raw categorical sequence
data are separable in both cases: every true taxon has a strict sample-level
gap under raw categorical Hamming distance, and spectral, OPTICS, and HDBSCAN
recover both cases exactly in the full benchmark.

The partial adaptive-TBS behavior comes from the adaptive pydiffmap distance
geometry and subsequent gate decisions, not from intrinsically inseparable raw
phylogenetic samples.

## Key Points

- `phylo_large_32taxa` reproduces the full benchmark row exactly in the focused
  run: ARI `0.977001`, `43` predicted clusters, homogeneity `1.0`,
  completeness `0.974066`. There are no mixed final clusters. The loss is
  fragmentation: `3` true taxa split, with `11` extra fragments beyond the
  true `32` taxa.
- In `phylo_large_32taxa`, raw categorical Hamming has a strict gap for all
  `32` taxa. Adaptive pydiffmap keeps strict gaps for `30` taxa, but collapses
  taxa `9` and `31` to zero between-taxon distance; taxon `31` is consequently
  scattered into singletons.
- `phylo_large_64taxa` remains partial. The full benchmark row records ARI
  `0.561151` with `61` clusters. The focused rerun on the current tree gives
  ARI `0.636215` with `60` clusters, stable across five immediate repeats, so
  the exact row differs from the archived full-run artifact but the failure
  mechanism is unchanged.
- In the focused `phylo_large_64taxa` run, raw categorical Hamming has a strict
  gap for all `64` taxa and nearest-neighbor label purity `1.0` through
  `k=5`. Adaptive pydiffmap keeps strict gaps for only `30` taxa; its nearest
  neighbor same-label fraction falls to `0.820312` at `k=1` and `0.771484` at
  `k=5`.
- The `64`-taxon focused run has `21` mixed final clusters containing `253`
  samples and `16` split true taxa. Mixed final boundaries usually have open
  child-parent edge gates but closed sibling gates; examples include a
  `40`-sample boundary mixing taxa `1`, `7`, `9`, `34`, and `62` with corrected
  sibling p-value `0.322168`, and a `24`-sample boundary mixing taxa `4`,
  `39`, and `45` with corrected sibling p-value `0.014866`.

## Evidence

- `benchmarks/results/focus_phylo_large_adaptive_20260627/summary.csv` records
  the focused rerun metrics and distance-separation counts.
- `benchmarks/results/focus_phylo_large_adaptive_20260627/*/distance_pair_summary.csv`
  and `distance_taxon_margins.csv` compare raw categorical Hamming, one-hot
  Hamming, and adaptive pydiffmap distances.
- `benchmarks/results/focus_phylo_large_adaptive_20260627/*/adaptive_*` files
  record assignments, crosstabs, mixed clusters, fragmentation, full
  annotations, and traversal p-value traces.
- `benchmarks/shared/cases/phylogenetic.py` defines the two large cases:
  `32` taxa over `500` categorical sites with `10` samples/taxon, and `64`
  taxa over `1000` sites with `8` samples/taxon.
- `benchmarks/shared/generators/generate_phylogenetic.py` shows that samples
  are generated by leaf sequences plus within-taxon drift, not by exact
  duplicate leaf sequences.
